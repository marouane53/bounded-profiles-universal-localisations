\begin{abstract}
For a regular sequence of positive even central elements we separate
boundedness of a derived Hom into vanishing on the punctured central
charts and a single test on the closed fibre. If the fibre detects
unbounded tensors of two perfect factors, the closed test is finite
generation of one individual cohomology module over the parameter ring.
A two-parameter example has detectors with identical free cohomology
over that ring and different bounded profiles. Results in this note
are proved; the script checks specific instances.
\end{abstract}

\section{A central support test}
Take a locally finite, nonnegatively graded associative algebra $A$
over a field, with zero differential. Suppose $s_1,\ldots,s_c$ are
positive even homogeneous central elements, each a non-zero-divisor
on the preceding quotient, with nonzero final quotient $G$.
Write $S=k[t_1,\ldots,t_c]$ acting by $t_i=s_i$, $|t_i|=|s_i|$.
Finiteness over $S$ refers to this specified action. Use perfect right
$A$-modules $P,E$, $Y=P^\vee=\RH_A(P,A)$ perfect left, and
$K=\RH_A(P,E)\simeq E\ten_A Y$. Bars denote derived reduction to $G$.
Properness of $A$ is unnecessary. For an augmented dg algebra $D$ with
$D_{\fd}(D)=\thick(k)$ and $\RH_D(k,k)\simeq A$, the equivalence
$\RH_D(k,-)$ carries its bounded profiles to these profiles on $\per A$.
Indeed the isomorphism on all shifted endomorphisms of $k$ extends by
triangles and summands to full faithfulness and gives essential image
$\per A$.

\begin{thm}\label{central}
A perfect source $P$ belongs to $\UU_E$ precisely when
\[
 E[s_i^{-1}]\ten_{A[s_i^{-1}]}Y[s_i^{-1}]\simeq0
 \quad(1\le i\le c),\qquad
 \dim_k H^*(\bar E\ten_G\bar Y)<\infty.                 \tag{1}
\]
Suppose in addition that for perfect right $M$ and left $N$ over $G$,
finite total $H^*(M\ten_G N)$ implies finite total $H^*M$ or $H^*N$.
Then the last condition in (1) is equivalent to
\[
                    H^*E\text{ is finite over }S
          \quad\text{or}\quad H^*Y\text{ is finite over }S .       \tag{2}
\]
In particular a closed fibre admitting the derivative of
\othernote{two_cuts}{A lower bound for derived tensor products}
satisfies the additional hypothesis when $H(G)$ is infinite; a
finite-dimensional $G$ satisfies it trivially.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
For any locally finite bounded-below $S$-complex $C$ let $C//t$ denote
the cone of $C[-|t|]\xrightarrow{t} C$. For one parameter, $H^*C$ is
finite-dimensional exactly when $C[t^{-1}]$ is acyclic and $H^*(C//t)$
is finite-dimensional. In fact finite cohomology is killed by a power
of $t$ and both implications in this direction follow. In the other
direction the long exact cohomology sequence of the cone makes $t$
bijective in sufficiently high degrees. In each such degree $H^*C$
embeds in its localization and hence vanishes. Local finiteness and
the lower bound finish the argument. Iterate this test: localizations
of iterated cones at $t_i$ vanish if $C[t_i^{-1}]$ vanishes, since
central localization is flat. From finite cohomology of the last cone
deduce finiteness of the preceding ones one at a time. Conversely the
iterated cones of a finite-cohomology complex have finite cohomology.

For finite cells $P,E$ the graded pieces of $K$ are locally finite and
bounded below. This property passes to their retracts on cohomology.
Localization commutes with perfect Hom and gives the first terms of
(1). At one regular central element, $A/(s)$ is represented by
$\Cone(s:A[-|s|]\to A)$ as an $A$-bimodule in the derived category;
both outer actions on the cone are legitimate because the degree is
even. Applying $\RH_A(P,-)$ to the multiplication triangle of $E$ and
then extension-restriction adjunction identifies $K//s$ with Hom of
the two reduced perfects over $A/(s)$. Reduction preserves perfect
duals, as is checked on free modules and then by thick closure.
Iteration over the regular sequence yields
$K//(s_1,\ldots,s_c)\simeq \bar E\ten_G\bar Y$ as $k$-complexes,
with the remaining central actions at each stage. This proves (1).

We record the graded Nakayama step. For $C$ as above,
$H^*(C//(t_1,\ldots,t_c))$ is finite-dimensional exactly when
$H^*C$ is finitely generated over $S$. If $H^*C$ is finite over $S$,
the cone sequence expresses its next cohomology as an extension of a
shifted kernel by a cokernel. Both are finite over $S/(t_1)$ by
noetherianity, and finiteness over the remaining polynomial ring suffices
for induction. Conversely apply induction to $C//t_1$. The quotient
$H^*C/t_1H^*C$ embeds in $H^*(C//t_1)$ and is finite over the
noetherian remaining polynomial ring. Lift homogeneous generators.
Induction on degree, using $|t_1|>0$ and the lower bound, proves that
they generate $H^*C$ over $S$. This also works when the parameter action
has a kernel. Apply the criterion separately to $E$ and $Y$, whose
iterated cones are their derived reductions on underlying complexes.
The fibre hypothesis gives necessity in (2). For sufficiency, tensor
a finite-cohomology fibre factor with a perfect factor, using cells and
retracts. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{What the hypotheses detect}
\paragraph{Proved obstruction to dropping the fibre.}
In $A=k[s,t,z]$ with variables of degree two, take
$E=A/(s,t)$, $P=A$. The regular sequence makes $E$ perfect. Both open
localizations of $K$ vanish but $H^*K=k[z]$ is infinite.
There is also an obstruction to replacing the fibre by (2) without
testing its Tor. Take $A=k[s,t,u,v]$, all degrees two, use parameters
$s,t$, set $E=A/(s,t,u)$ and take the dual of the perfect left
$Y=A/(v)$ as source. Neither cohomology module is finite over $k[s,t]$.
Their derived tensor is $k$ by the disjoint Koszul resolutions. On the
fibre $k[u,v]$ the two surviving directions meet only in finite total
Tor. The universal assertion (1) still applies.

\paragraph{Proved obstruction to ordinary central projective support.}
Set $A_0=k\langle x,z\rangle/(x^2z-zx^2)$ in generator degree one and
$A=A_0[t]$ with $t$ central of degree two. The sequence $s=x^2,t$
is regular. Its fibre $k\langle x,z\rangle/(x^2)$ has a free $z$.
Let $M_i=k[x]$ with $z$ acting by $i x$, $i=0,1$, and zero
differential. Each is perfect over $A_0$: set $u=z-i x$ and present
its kernel in $A_0$ freely by the two initial blocks $u,xu$ using
the words avoiding $xxu$. Let $E_i=M_i\otimes_k k[t]$. Both have
identical graded cohomology as $k[s,t]$-modules, free on generators in
degrees zero and one. Take $P$ to be the exterior tensor product of
$\Cone(z:A_0[-1]\to A_0)$ with $k[t]/(t)$. The Hom complex into $E_i$
is the tensor over $k$ of the Hom complexes of these two factors,
by finite cells. The second is a shift of $k$. In the first, precomposition
by $z$ is zero for $i=0$ and an isomorphism in high degrees for $i=1$.
Thus $P\in\UU_{E_1}$ and $P\notin\UU_{E_0}$, in all characteristics.
Even the full central modules of these detectors, hence their supports
on $\operatorname{Proj} k[s,t]$, lose this distinction.

\paragraph{Interpretation and priority.}
The cone and graded Nakayama steps are standard central-operator
arguments. \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220000090}{Avramov--Buchweitz}
describe pair supports over commutative complete intersections;
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702654}{Avramov--Iyengar} realise
closed subsets for central cohomological operations subject to finiteness
and vanishing hypotheses. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12009}{Pollitz}
constructs supports for pairs of dg modules over Koszul complexes.
The extra condition (2) here is a consequence of rigidity in a possibly
noncommutative closed fibre; neither global finite generation over the
centre nor a tensor product formula for individual central supports
is assumed. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0642}{BIK stratification}
and \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.06773}{BIKP tensor-ideal
classification} concern categories with different structural hypotheses.
Equation (1) describes bounded Hom. It does not assert a classification
of all localising subcategories or construct a projective scheme from
an arbitrary sequence. A full general poset classification for several
parameters is open in this work.
